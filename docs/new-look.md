# What's new in the new look

A tour of everything in the redesign: what each part does, where to find it, and how to use it. It follows the order the pieces could come in: music, settings, sidebar and screens, the ride screen, then the glass look. After those come the Zwift Click, the 3D rider, and what isn't finished or tested yet.

## 1. Music

Play your own music while you ride, without another app open.

- **Your music folder:** in Settings › Sound & music, choose the folder your MP3s are in.
  - VeloForge plays them from there, folders inside it too, without copying them.
  - It looks through the folder in the background each time it opens, or when you press **Check for new songs**. New songs go at the end of the list, and songs deleted from the folder leave it.
  - If the folder isn't there (a drive not plugged in), its songs are greyed out until it's back.
  - **Hide** takes a song out of the list without touching the file. **Show hidden** puts them back.
- **Adding songs one by one:** **+ Add songs…** (or dropping MP3s onto the window) copies them into VeloForge's data folder, in the background. **Remove** deletes that copy.
- **The list:** each song shows its name and artist, and whether it's from the folder or added. Drag a song by the dots on its left to change the order.
- **Playing:** songs play one after another, or shuffled, on the computer's speakers. A play bar on the card plays, pauses and skips. While shuffling, Previous goes back to the song you heard before.
- **While riding:** a small "Now playing" panel on the ride screen shows the song, with play/pause, next and previous.
- **From the bike:** the Zwift Click's buttons control the music. By default:
  - **Y** plays and pauses.
  - **A** skips to the next song.
  - **Z** goes back.

  To change them, in Settings › Sound & music, choose an action, then press the Click button you want for it.

## 2. Settings

Settings is now a sidebar of pages instead of one long list. Your profile sits at the top, showing your FTP, weight and W/kg.

| Page | What's on it |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | Your rider in 3D (below), then FTP, heart rates, weights and your zones. |
| **Riding** | Trainer difficulty, units, speed, auto-start, drafting, workouts, aero drag and rolling resistance, and your gearing for virtual shifting. |
| **Appearance** | The picture behind the screens (or your own), panel darkness, text size, numbers and the quote on Home; then the 3D view: renderer, detail, resolution, frame rate, screen, time of day and weather. |
| **Sound & music** | Your music and which Click buttons control it, then the workout beeps. |
| **Connections** | Strava, intervals.icu and the phone remote. |
| **Data & about** | The data folder, remembered devices, updates and the version. |

### Your rider (Settings › Profile)

- **Preview:** you on the bike in 3D, pedalling slowly on a soft floor.
  - Drag it to turn it, or use **Side**, **¾** and **Front**.
  - It draws only the rider and bike, not the world, so it's light on the graphics card.
- **Jersey colour:** nine swatches, or any colour. The rainbow-ringed button opens a colour wheel:
  - Click or drag on the wheel to pick the hue and strength.
  - A slider sets the brightness.
  - A box takes a hex code (`#1E4FA8`, `1E4FA8` or the short `#14A`).
  - Click away or press Esc to close it.
- **Bike:** Aero road or Classic, with paint colours for the aero bike. This only shows when the build has the aero bike (see section 7).
- **Kit:** helmet, gloves, socks and shoes, each black or white. On black shoes the dial and trim turn grey so they still show.
- **Skin tone:** six tones.

Every change shows in the preview straight away, and on your rider in the 3D world.

### Your gearing (Settings › Riding)

Set virtual shifting up like your own bike, so the gears feel the same:

- **Chainrings:** one ring (1×) or two (2×), with their teeth.
- **Cassette:** pick from a list of common ones (checked against the makers' specs, including SRAM XPLR 13-speed 10–46), or type your own, smallest cog to biggest.
- **Wheel and tyre:** 700×25c to 700×45c, 650b×47 and 29×2.2. Bigger tyres go a little further each turn of the pedals.
- **The result:** a row of bars, one per gear, with the easiest and hardest speeds at 90 rpm.

With two rings, the gears follow the order a rider shifts in: the small ring up the cassette, then the big ring. Crossed gears and near-duplicates are left out. Rides start in the middle gear. The Devices screen shows the current gear on the same kind of ladder.

## 3. Sidebar and screens

- **The menu:** a vertical bar of labelled round buttons on the left: Home, Library, Calendar, Activity, then Devices and Settings. It hides while you ride.
- **Home:**
  - Today's planned workout, with one button to start it.
  - This week's rides, and what's coming up.
  - The ride bar at the bottom: pick a workout or free ride, and a route.
  - A line about cycling each day, chosen to suit the day: a hard workout, an easy one, a rest day, a test, or a run of rides in a row. The refresh button shows another. You can turn it off in Appearance.
- **Library:** all the workouts, and the routes.
  - Sceneries are listed down the side, each with its own icon: a barn for Hills, a lakeside, peaks for Alpine, the coast, a torii gate for Japan.
  - Loop routes are marked with a loop icon.
- **Calendar:** your planned and finished rides on a calendar.
- **Activity:**
  - Your totals down the side.
  - The chosen ride in full: figures, chart, zones, best efforts. Your earlier rides are listed under it.
  - Your trends and records.
- **Devices:**
  - Equal-sized cards for the trainer, power, heart rate, cadence and the Click.
  - Virtual shifting, with the gear ladder.
  - The simulated trainer.
  - The live data and the log.

## 4. The ride screen

- **Layout:** glass panels along the top over the 3D view. The ride's controls are in the centre panel, and the strip along the bottom has no panels so the road stays in view.
- **Starting by pedalling:** start pedalling and a 10-second countdown appears, then the ride starts by itself, with no button to reach for. Stopping pedalling during the countdown calls it off. You can switch this off in Settings › Riding ("Auto-start").
- **Finishing:**
  - Hold the Click's **Y** for 3 seconds. A ring fills while you hold.
  - A short press of Y still does whatever Y is set to, such as play/pause.
  - Clicking **Finish** asks too.
  - Either way, "Finish the ride?" appears: **Y** (or the button) finishes, **B** carries on.

## 5. The glass look

- **Panels:** frosted glass panels over a picture.
- **Section colours:** each part of the app gets its own colour of the picture, so you always know where you are.
- **The picture:** "Waves" by default (a photo from Unsplash), or your own pictures, added in Settings › Appearance.
- **Panel darkness:** adjustable, for pictures that are bright or busy.

## 6. Zwift Click

The Click can now run the whole app, so you don't need a keyboard or mouse on the bike.

- **Shifting:** **+** and **−** shift gears during a ride.
- **Getting around (everywhere except the ride screen):**
  - A white ring shows the selected control.
  - The left half's pad moves it: up, down, left and right.
  - **+** presses what's lit, and **−** steps back towards the menu.
  - Without the left half, **Z**, **A** and **Y** move the ring left, right and up.
  - The arrow keys and Enter on a keyboard do the same.
- **Home lists:** while the workout or route list is lit, ▲ and ▼ step through it.
- **The left half without Zwift:**
  - The left half normally waits to be unlocked by Zwift, and after about 75 seconds it drops its connection.
  - VeloForge answers the simple unlock reply and keeps the Click awake. When the left half is locked, VeloForge reconnects it quietly about once a minute.
  - There's a switch for this in Devices.
  - VeloForge never answers Zwift's encryption challenge.
- **Battery:** the newer Click reports its battery in a different place, and VeloForge now reads it.

## 7. The 3D rider and bike

- **The body:**
  - Fuller arms and hands that grip the hoods.
  - A face with a nose, brow, lips and jaw.
  - Road shoes with a pointed toe, a dark collar and heel, a stiff sole and a cleat.
  - Ankles that sit where a real ankle does, so the heel shows behind the leg.
- **The aero road bike:**
  - VeloForge can draw an aero road bike with disc brakes, matte black wheels, and paint in any colour.
  - It is baked from a Blender model by `tools/bike-model/bake.py`. The README there explains how.
  - **The model isn't in this repository:** the one it was made for is personal-use only. A build without the baked files rides the classic bike, and Settings › Profile doesn't offer the choice.
  - To use it, bake a bike you're allowed to share into `assets/models/` and build again.

## Not finished, or not tested yet

- **Platforms:** tested on macOS only, not yet on Linux or Windows, and not measured on the MX250.
  - The aero bike adds about 1.3 ms of CPU a frame on the Mac. The Classic bike is the lighter choice.
  - The Profile preview hasn't been timed on the MX250.
- **Click navigation:** the pad doesn't move round the ride screen itself, where + and − shift instead.
- **Music:** MP3s only, played by VeloForge itself. Controlling Spotify or Apple Music already running on the computer (play/pause, next, previous, the song's name), as suggested, would be a good next step.
